## A safe weak-sector analysis path

1. Select completed, server-verified **individual** training sessions only. Exclude Crew, imported duplicates, reference runs and hypothetical branches from individual learning evidence. Repeated decisions on one contact count once.
2. Derive the sector from the reported position at the first working-sensor observation, using the recorded scenario/commands and the correct historical engine. Current `Evidence` contains range and `first_detectable_tick`, not bearing. Spawn bearing and hidden ideal truth must not stand in for the observation. Define the bearing convention and use the reported, quantised position where applicable.
3. Measure designation delay after first actual detectability; no designation is a censored/missed observation, not an invented infinite numeric delay. Keep unassessable contacts separate.
4. Compare like contexts: difficulty, visibility/night, sensor failures, contact range and raid family can confound slow designations. Require exposure in at least two comparable sectors, at least three assessable contacts and at least two independent sessions per reported sector; these are transparent guardrails, not validated statistical thresholds.
5. Present observed counts, median delays, censoring and context as descriptive evidence. Show **Insufficient comparable evidence** instead of naming a weakest sector when coverage is inadequate. Do not interpret an estimate as a measured attention deficit.
6. Only after tests and review, add automatic selection behind a **new Director metadata version** with frozen sample inclusion, sector summaries, source revisions and explanation. Preserve v2.1 snapshots and replay/export/import behavior unchanged. An officer can still choose a supplied sector deliberately today.

## Difficulty evaluation boundary

The source code implements four-skill Beta posteriors and a bounded seeded curriculum heuristic, not fitted family-level success calibration. A valid next evaluation must freeze predictions before outcomes, define success per skill, hold out learners/sessions and compare baseline prediction errors and reliability bins with denominators. Use the existing study/data interfaces where their contracts fit; do not silently reinterpret radar classification metrics as trainee success calibration.

The supplied audit's 40–50 drills can support a descriptive software/usability sample. They do not automatically establish transportable difficulty, retention or improved learning. No model should be labelled calibrated at a fixed outcome count. [Scikit-learn's calibration documentation](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/calibration.html) distinguishes probability reliability from fitting and explains the need for independent calibration data; it also notes that a lower Brier score alone does not prove better calibration.
