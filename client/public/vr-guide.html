01 / THE IDEA

What are we actually building?

Virtual reality (VR) means a headset shows a computer-made world in front of your eyes. Each eye gets its own view. As you turn your head, the view changes. Controllers let you point at things and select them.

VYUHA’s VR version places you at a fixed observation post above the existing training terrain. A control console sits in front of you. You look at the simulated aerial contacts, read the available sensor evidence, and record your decisions.

This uses WebXR: the browser’s connection to a compatible headset. There is no separate headset app to install. The headset browser opens VYUHA, asks for permission, and starts the immersive view.

Ordinary VYUHAVR observation post
A screen, mouse and ordinary web controls.A stereo headset view, head tracking and controller rays.
The observation camera can focus on a selected contact.Your head controls where you look. Selecting a contact highlights it without turning your head for you.
Radar and other evidence appear in panels.Detected contacts and available radar, IR, RF and IFF evidence appear on console surfaces inside VR.
Exercises run in the browser simulation worker.The same worker runs the exercise and accepts the same action messages.
The server reconstructs decisions and verifies scores.The same server verification and after-action review apply.

Why sit at a fixed post?

The app does not push you around, fly the camera or use joystick movement. This gives the experience a stable reference and a simple control scheme. It is intended for seated use. That design choice still needs a comfort trial with a real headset.

The terrain, aircraft shapes, sensor behaviour and response rules are illustrative training models. VR makes the presentation immersive; it does not make these models operationally accurate.
02 / TRY IT NOW

Your first laptop preview

  1. Start VYUHA as usual. From the project folder, run bash scripts/start-local.sh. Initial setup may need Internet. If a server is already running, use that server or stop it before reusing its port.
  2. Open http://127.0.0.1:8000 in your browser. Choose Training ground.
  3. Find the VR observation post card below the graphics settings. Select Open VR cockpit.
  4. The message will normally say no immersive headset was detected. This is expected. Enter VR stays disabled; the preview and its controls still work.
  5. Select Begin exercise inside the cockpit or under Desktop preview controls.
  6. Select a contact such as T2. Follow the designation, classification and response steps on the next pages.
  7. Use Pause exercise whenever you need time. After 75 seconds of running time, the ordinary review opens. Wait for Server verified.

Two ways to click

You can click the drawn console buttons, or use the normal buttons below the preview. These buttons are also usable by keyboard. Both send the same actions.

Choose the exercise before entering a headset

The normal setup controls still select the trainee, day/night conditions, saved exercise or adaptive drill. An adaptive drill may first show Prepare adaptive drill, then Begin exercise. Preparation requires the local server. Assigned exercises can be accepted using the existing workspace controls.

What if the server is off?

The original scripted exercise can fall back to device-only practice. Decisions can be saved in browser storage; the review says verification is pending. Advanced/adaptive and library exercises require their original local server. “Saved” and “server verified” are different states.

Desktop preview checks the software and lets you learn the workflow. It cannot show you what headset lenses, controller weight or motion comfort feel like.
03 / READ THE CONSOLE

Three panels, one training picture

TermWhat it means
Contact / T1, T2…A simulated object with a tracking label. A label does not tell you whether it is hostile.
RadarA modelled position-detection sensor. NORMAL, DEGRADED and OFFLINE describe its current availability. The selected contact can still have no radar return at that moment.
IR / thermalThe modelled infrared sensor and illustrative heat-style view. This is not a real thermal camera.
RFRadio-frequency evidence. “Link detected” is a cue about a radio link. “No link detected” is not proof of identity.
IFFIdentification friend or foe. A “friendly reply” is a simulated cue; “no reply” does not automatically mean hostile.
DeclaredThe type and identity you recorded. Unknown means you have not classified it.

Four contact buttons fit on a page inside VR. The contact-page button cycles through more. Contacts appear in the list after a working sensor has detected them. Sensor outages are shown as OFFLINE, rather than a made-up negative reading.

04 / THE DECISION LOOP

Observe → designate → classify → respond

  1. Select a detected contact. Point at its label and release the trigger, or click it on your laptop. The left panel shows the evidence for that contact.
  2. Designate. This records that you chose to examine the contact. Classification stays disabled until designation.
  3. Choose its type. The Type button cycles through Quadcopter, Fixed-wing, No-RF FPV and Bird. The choice is your answer; the app does not fill in the correct answer.
  4. Choose its identity. The Identity button cycles through Hostile, Friendly and Neutral. Select Confirm classification to submit both choices.
  5. Choose a response. Select Hold fire, RF jammer or Kinetic response, where the exercise permits it. This first selection arms a pending response; it does not execute it.
  6. Check the target and confirm. The confirmation button names the response and contact. Select it to send the decision. Select Cancel to clear it. A pending response expires after eight seconds and clears when the selected contact or exercise phase changes.
  7. Read the exercise log. It reports what happened under the model’s rules. Continue observing and choosing contacts.
ResponseMeaning inside this prototype
Hold fireRecord a decision to keep monitoring. It does not pause time or remove the contact.
RF jammerA modelled radio-link response. It can fail for a no-RF contact or outside the permitted range.
Kinetic responseA modelled physical response. Range and restricted-zone rules still apply.

Example: Suppose the cues lead you to classify T2 as a friendly quadcopter. You designate it, select Quadcopter + Friendly, confirm the classification, select Hold fire, then confirm Hold on T2. That is an example of the controls, not a supplied answer for every scenario.

A button being enabled means you may submit that decision. It does not mean the decision is correct or effective. The shared simulation applies the rules and the server later verifies the recorded result.
05 / WHEN A HEADSET IS AVAILABLE

Connect it to the same project

You need a headset/browser combination that supports immersive WebXR, controller selection, and WebGL. Meta Quest browser with tracked controllers is the software emulator profile used here; this is not a claim of a physical Quest test or of support for every headset. Hand-only control and phone cardboard viewers are outside this version’s tested scope.

The important difference: HTTPS

Browsers protect access to headset hardware. A laptop’s http://127.0.0.1 address can be treated as trustworthy locally. On a headset, 127.0.0.1 means the headset itself. To reach your laptop, use its LAN address over HTTPS with a certificate trusted by the headset.

A certificate is the website’s digital identity. A self-signed certificate or clicking past a warning may not make WebXR available. The certificate must cover the exact hostname or IP address you open, and its issuing authority must be trusted on the headset. Have the person managing your network set that up if it is unfamiliar.

Launch with the existing HTTPS support

After normal project setup, put your certificate and private key paths into these variables. These are placeholders; substitute your actual files.

export VYUHA_SSL_CERTFILE=/absolute/path/server-cert.pem
export VYUHA_SSL_KEYFILE=/absolute/path/server-key.pem
bash scripts/start-vr.sh --build
  1. Put laptop and headset on the same trusted network. Allow the chosen server port through the laptop’s firewall.
  2. Open the printed https://…:8000 LAN URL in the headset browser. The page should have no certificate warning.
  3. Join the unit using a callsign if prompted. Open Training ground → Open VR cockpit.
  4. Sit with clear space for your hands. Use the headset’s normal boundary/recentre controls to face forward.
  5. Select Enter VR and allow the browser’s permission prompt. Point either controller at a console button and release its trigger to select.

The launcher enforces certificate configuration and uses existing Unit Mode. It does not generate or install certificates. Keep this prototype on a trusted network; it has no production authentication.

06 / KEEP CONTROL OF THE SESSION

Pause, leave, and recover

Pause exercise stops simulation time. Resume exercise continues it. You can do both inside VR. The headset’s system menu can also end the immersive session.

Leaving VR pauses an active drill. When WebXR reports hidden or blurred visibility, the app also pauses. Returning to visibility does not automatically resume. When the exercise ends normally, VR closes and the ordinary after-action review opens. Review, exercise authoring and unit administration remain ordinary web pages.

Your first real trial should be short

Check text clarity, controller aiming and the pause/exit buttons before running a full drill. Stop if you feel uncomfortable. Keep walking and joystick movement out of the trial. Record the headset, browser version, comfort observations and performance instead of assuming the emulator proves them.

07 / WHAT THE CHECKS PROVE

Evidence, with clear boundaries

The validation report at docs/vr/VALIDATION.md and the raw browser results at docs/vr/evidence/browser-results.json record the checks for this version.

CheckWhat it establishes
Frontend and backend regressionsThe automated suites pass, including existing simulation parity checks. This protects the shared training behaviour.
VR action testsDecisions obey selection/phase rules, preserve available evidence, and replay through the ordinary scoring engine.
XR lifecycle testsDenial, failed setup, interrupted entry, visibility loss, exit and re-entry have defined cleanup behaviour.
Desktop browser testThe cockpit renders, its controls work, and submitted decisions reach a server-verified review.
IWER emulated headset testA WebXR session renders through the real Three.js integration; two simulated controller rays operate scene buttons. A full-duration drill completes and is verified.
Physical headset / comfort / device FPSNot tested. Real lenses, tracking, display timing, controller ergonomics and sustained device performance require hardware.

The test browser uses software graphics on this laptop. Its speed is not a Quest performance measurement. Existing desktop/Android FPS figures from earlier releases also do not establish VR frame rate.

Before calling it ready for a particular headset

  1. Record device, browser, server and certificate setup.
  2. Confirm correct eye height, head tracking, readable text and both controller rays.
  3. Try designation, classification, response confirmation, timeout, pause, headset removal/system menu and re-entry.
  4. Finish several drills, including a sensor outage and an adaptive/library exercise. Check server verification and replay.
  5. Measure sustained headset frame rate and session behaviour under the intended workload. Record comfort feedback and any dropped tracking.
Passing a software emulator is useful evidence. It cannot honestly prove that every headset is comfortable, fast, compatible or ready for deployment.
08 / FOR THE PERSON MAINTAINING IT

One simulation, two presentations

Ordinary workspace OR VR console
               ↓ same action payloads
Browser worker: simulation + accepted decisions
               ↓ ordered event log
Local server: reconstruct and verify score
               ↓
Existing after-action review / replay / training profile

VRCockpit.tsx draws the console and reuses the shared world. Console labels are textures on real 3D surfaces, so headset controls do not depend on a browser HTML overlay. Both controllers use rays to intersect those surfaces.

vr.ts contains action gating, observed evidence and session lifecycle handling. The browser must report immersive-vr support. A user click requests a local reference space; a fixed 1.6 m rig offset puts the seated origin at the console. No camera-follow routine runs during VR.

The worker stays at 30 Hz. WebXR renders independently at the device’s display cadence. VR uses the low-detail world, pixel ratio 1, a requested 0.85 framebuffer scale and maximum foveation where supported. These are conservative rendering settings, not measured device-performance guarantees. The ordinary observation renderer is removed while the VR preview is open.
